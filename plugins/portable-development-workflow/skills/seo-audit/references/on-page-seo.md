# On-Page SEO & Content Optimization Reference

## Overview

On-page SEO encompasses everything you control on your website: content quality, keyword targeting, HTML elements, internal linking, and content structure. Modern on-page optimization is less about keyword density and more about intent matching, semantic depth, entity clarity, accessibility, and creating content that humans and machines can understand.

## Keyword Research & Strategy

### Intent-First Approach
Keywords still matter, but intent matters more. Every keyword maps to one of four intent types:
- **Informational**: User wants to learn ("what is [topic]")
- **Navigational**: User wants a specific brand, product, account, or page ("[brand] login")
- **Commercial Investigation**: User is comparing options ("best [category] in [market]" or "[product] vs [alternative]")
- **Transactional**: User is ready to act ("[service] quote [location]" or "buy [product]")

Match content format to intent: informational queries need guides/explainers, commercial queries need comparisons/reviews, transactional queries need service/product pages with clear CTAs.

### Keyword Types to Target
- **Long-tail commercial intent keywords**: Usually clearer about buyer stage and conversion intent
- **LSI (Latent Semantic Indexing) keywords**: Related terms that establish topical depth (e.g., for "cybersecurity": threat detection, vulnerability assessment, incident response, zero trust)
- **Comparison and modifier keywords**: "best," "vs," "top," "near me," "cost," "quote," "reviews," and current-year modifiers when genuinely relevant
- **Question-based keywords**: Map directly to People Also Ask and AI-generated answers
- **Entity-based keywords**: Brand names, product names, technology names that establish entity relationships

### Topical Authority
- Build content clusters around core topics — not isolated blog posts
- Create pillar pages that comprehensively cover broad topics, with supporting content for subtopics
- Entity-level optimization matters more than volume: prove you're the authority through depth, not breadth
- Interlink cluster content bidirectionally — pillar ↔ supporting pages
- Cover topics from every relevant angle: definitions, how-tos, comparisons, case studies, FAQs

## Title Tags

- Keep under 60 characters to avoid truncation (Google measures by pixel width, ~580px)
- Front-load primary keyword — search engines weight the beginning of titles more heavily
- Include brand name at the end when possible: "Primary Keyword | Brand Name"
- Make titles compelling — CTR is a signal. Use personality, specifics, and curiosity
- Use natural, compelling language. Avoid titles that read like keyword lists.
- Each page needs a unique title tag — duplicate titles confuse search engines and users
- Avoid keyword stuffing: one primary keyword per title

## Meta Descriptions

- Keep under 155 characters (Google may truncate longer descriptions)
- Include primary keyword naturally — Google bolds matching terms
- Write as a compelling ad for the page — this is your click pitch
- Include a clear value proposition and/or call-to-action
- Unique per page — duplicate meta descriptions are a wasted opportunity
- Google often rewrites meta descriptions; well-written ones get used more often

## Header Hierarchy (H1-H6)

- One H1 per page, containing the primary keyword naturally
- Use H2s for major sections, H3s for subsections — logical, nested structure
- Headers should accurately describe the content beneath them
- Include keywords and related terms in headers naturally
- Clear heading structure helps AI systems identify which section answers which question
- Avoid skipping levels (H1 → H3 without H2)

## Content Quality & E-E-A-T

### E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness)
Use E-E-A-T as a practical quality lens, especially for pages where trust, safety, money, health, legal, or operational decisions are involved:

**Experience**: Show firsthand knowledge
- Include real examples, case studies, original data
- Use real images (not stock photos) when possible
- Reference specific projects, clients, outcomes
- "We implemented X for Y client and achieved Z result" > "X is important for businesses"

**Expertise**: Demonstrate deep knowledge
- Author bios with real credentials, linked to professional profiles
- Cite industry data, reference standards, use accurate terminology
- Cover topics comprehensively — don't leave obvious gaps
- Publish content by subject matter experts, not generic writers

**Authoritativeness**: Be recognized by others
- Earn mentions in industry publications, podcasts, community discussions
- Build backlinks from relevant, authoritative sources
- Maintain consistent entity information across the web
- Third-party validation (awards, certifications, partnerships) strengthens authority

**Trustworthiness**: Be transparent and reliable
- Clear about/contact pages with real business information
- Physical address, phone number, email visible on site
- Privacy policy, terms of service
- HTTPS, clean security record
- Accurate, up-to-date information — content freshness matters

### Content Depth & Quality
- Target 1,500-2,500+ words for pillar content; match depth to topic complexity and intent
- Use original research, data, and insights — information gain is a real signal
- Avoid thin content pages (<300 words) with no unique value
- Update existing content regularly — content freshness directly affects rankings
- Every piece should add something new that isn't already on page 1 of search results
- Write for humans first, optimize for search engines second
- Straightforward language with low cognitive load performs best

## Internal Linking

- Every new piece of content should link to important service pages and key guides
- Distribute link equity from high-authority pages to pages that need ranking support
- Use descriptive, keyword-relevant anchor text — not "click here" or "learn more"
- Contextual links within body content carry more weight than footer/sidebar links
- Create hub-and-spoke structures: pillar pages link to cluster content and vice versa
- Audit internal links quarterly — fix broken links, update anchors, add new relevant links
- Important pages should have the most internal links pointing to them
- Aim for every important page to be reachable within 3 clicks from homepage

## Image Optimization

- Compress images: use WebP or AVIF formats for best quality/size ratio
- Use descriptive filenames: `primary-service-location.jpg` not `IMG_1234.jpg`
- Write descriptive alt text that includes relevant keywords naturally
- Specify width/height attributes to prevent CLS
- Implement lazy loading for images below the fold
- Use responsive images (srcset) for different device sizes
- Original images > stock photos for E-E-A-T signals

## Content Freshness

- Update high-performing content quarterly with new data, examples, and current information
- Add "Last updated" dates visible on the page
- Refresh title tags and meta descriptions when content is updated
- Republish with updated dates only when substantial changes are made
- Monitor for content decay: pages that were ranking but are now declining need refreshing
- Outdated statistics, broken links, and stale references hurt credibility

## Voice & AI Search Optimization

- Under each question heading, include a short 1-2 sentence answer that AI tools can easily extract
- Use FAQ sections with clear question-answer format
- Structure content with direct, concise answers followed by detailed explanations
- Natural conversational language works better for voice search
- Target question-based keywords ("how," "what," "why," "when," "who")

## Content Formatting Best Practices

- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences) for readability
- Use bullet points and numbered lists for scannable information
- Include a table of contents for long-form content
- Bold key terms and phrases for emphasis and scannability
- Use visual breaks: images, charts, callout boxes, pull quotes
- Embed relevant video (especially YouTube) for engagement, dwell time, and AI citations
- Include interactive elements where relevant: calculators, tools, templates
