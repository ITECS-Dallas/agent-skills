# Local SEO Reference

## Overview

Local SEO optimizes a business's online presence for location-based discovery: map packs, business profiles, local organic results, directories, review platforms, and AI-generated local answers. It matters most for companies with physical locations, service areas, appointment-based services, local delivery, or market-specific demand.

## Google Business Profile (GBP)

For Google-driven markets, Google Business Profile is usually the central local SEO asset. For other markets or platforms, adapt the same completeness and consistency principles to the relevant local profiles.

### Complete Optimization Checklist
- **Business name**: Exact legal business name — no keyword stuffing
- **Categories**: Select the most specific primary category; add all relevant secondary categories
- **Address**: Exact, consistent address matching all other citations
- **Phone number**: Local phone number preferred over toll-free
- **Hours**: Accurate hours including special hours for holidays
- **Website URL**: Link to the most relevant landing page (homepage or location page)
- **Description**: Use full 750 characters; include primary keywords, services, and service area naturally
- **Services/Products**: Add all services with descriptions
- **Attributes**: Complete all relevant attributes (wheelchair accessible, free Wi-Fi, etc.)
- **Photos & Videos**: High-quality images of business, team, work, office. Add new photos regularly (weekly if possible)
- **Posts**: Publish updates at least twice weekly — events, offers, news, updates. Fresh content is a ranking signal
- **Q&A**: Proactively add common questions and answers
- **Messaging**: Enable and respond promptly

### GBP Ranking Factors
- **Proximity**: Distance from searcher to business location (you can't control this, but it's the #1 factor)
- **Relevance**: How well your profile matches the search query — complete profiles with accurate categories win
- **Prominence**: How well-known and trusted the business is — reviews, citations, backlinks, brand mentions

## NAP Consistency (Name, Address, Phone)

- Business information should be consistent across directories, citation platforms, maps, social profiles, and the website
- Even small variations (St. vs Street, Suite vs Ste.) can reduce trust signals
- Audit NAP consistency across top directories: Google, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook, industry-specific directories
- Use a consistent format everywhere and update all listings when information changes
- Inconsistent listings actively harm local rankings

## Citations & Directories

### Priority Directories (Build These First)
1. Google Business Profile
2. Bing Places for Business
3. Apple Maps Connect
4. Yelp
5. Facebook Business Page
6. Industry-specific directories (for IT services: Clutch, G2, UpCity, etc.)
7. Local chamber of commerce
8. BBB (Better Business Bureau)
9. Yellow Pages / local directories
10. Data aggregators: Factual (now Foursquare), Neustar Localeze, Acxiom

### Citation Quality > Quantity
- Prioritize authoritative, relevant directories over volume
- Industry-specific directories carry more weight than generic ones
- Local directories (city chamber, local business associations) build local authority
- Clean up duplicate listings — they cause confusion and dilute signals

## Reviews & Reputation Management

### Why Reviews Matter
- Reviews heavily influence local rankings and appear prominently in local packs and AI summaries
- Google uses review quality, recency, and keyword content to determine relevance
- Reviews are a form of user-generated content that reinforces service offerings
- AI systems pull from reviews to generate local business recommendations

### Review Strategy
- Ask satisfied customers for reviews after positive experiences — timing matters
- Make the review process easy: provide direct links to your Google review page
- Respond to every review — positive and negative — within 24-48 hours
- Positive review responses: genuine, specific appreciation
- Negative review responses: professional, empathetic, solution-oriented (demonstrates commitment)
- Encourage customers to mention specific services in reviews naturally (don't script reviews)
- Never buy fake reviews or script deceptive reviews.
- Google now allows anonymous reviews (users can change their public-facing name/image), which may increase review volume

### Review Monitoring
- Set up alerts for new reviews across all platforms
- Track review velocity (reviews per month) — consistency matters
- Monitor review sentiment for service quality insights
- Respond to reviews on all platforms, not just Google

## Local Content Strategy

### Location-Specific Landing Pages
- Create dedicated pages for each service in each geographic area
- Structure: `/services/[service-name]/[city-or-area]/`
- Each page needs unique, substantial content — not just city name swapped in a template
- Include local context: references to local landmarks, neighborhoods, community, regulations
- Add location-specific testimonials and case studies
- Include embedded Google Map
- Add LocalBusiness schema markup

### Hyper-Local Targeting
- Target specific neighborhoods, zip codes, and street names
- Create content that resonates with local audiences using local language and references
- Cover local events, news, and community involvement
- Partner with local organizations and businesses for content co-creation

### Content Types That Drive Local Authority
- Local industry guides and resources
- Case studies featuring local clients (with permission)
- Community involvement posts
- Local event coverage and sponsorship
- "Best of" local resource lists
- Local market data and insights

## Local Link Building

- Sponsor local events, charities, or organizations for directory listings and links
- Join and actively participate in local chamber of commerce
- Partner with complementary local businesses for cross-referrals and links
- Contribute to local publications or news outlets
- Get listed on local "best of" lists and awards
- Participate in local business associations and industry groups
- Create locally relevant resources that others want to link to

## Multi-Platform Local Presence

### Social Media for Local SEO
- Maintain active profiles on Facebook, LinkedIn, Instagram (platform choice depends on industry)
- Google monitors engagement across social channels as signals of prominence and brand authority
- Use location tags and local hashtags consistently
- Engage with local community: respond to comments, join local groups, share local content
- Social signals have visible impact on Bing rankings specifically

### Other Platforms
- Yelp: Claim and optimize profile; respond to all reviews
- Apple Maps: Ensure accurate listing via Apple Maps Connect
- Bing Places: Complete profile; Bing's local results use different ranking factors than Google
- Nextdoor: Claim business page for hyperlocal visibility
- Industry platforms: Clutch, G2, etc. for B2B service businesses

## Local SEO for AI & Voice Search

- Optimize GBP with complete, accurate information — AI pulls from GBP for instant answers
- Structure FAQ pages with clear question-answer format for voice search extraction
- Include "near me" variations naturally in content
- Ensure schema markup (LocalBusiness, Service, FAQ) is implemented correctly
- Voice search queries are more conversational — optimize for natural language patterns
- AI and map experiences increasingly rely on consistent local entity information, visible website content, reviews, and structured data where appropriate.

## Measurement

- **Google Business Profile Insights**: Discovery searches, direct searches, actions taken
- **Local pack rankings**: Track positions for target keywords in map pack results
- **Website analytics**: Traffic from local searches, location-specific page performance
- **Call tracking**: Measure phone inquiries from local search
- **Direction requests**: Track Google Maps direction requests
- **Review metrics**: Volume, average rating, response rate, sentiment
- **Citation accuracy score**: Track NAP consistency across directories

## Common Local SEO Mistakes

- Keyword-stuffing the business name in GBP
- Inconsistent NAP across directories
- Ignoring or not responding to reviews
- Template-based location pages with no unique content
- Not claiming Bing Places or Apple Maps listings
- Neglecting Google Business Profile posts and updates
- Using a PO Box or virtual office address (violates Google guidelines for non-service-area businesses)
- Not setting service areas correctly for service-area businesses
